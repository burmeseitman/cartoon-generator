# Cartoon Style Guide

This document captures the visual style of a Burmese cartoonist. The environment and characters adapt to the news topic, but the core art style stays consistent.

---

## 1. Core Art Style (Always Fixed)

- **Hand-drawn Burmese cartoon illustration** — ink line art with watercolor coloring
- **Bold black ink outlines** — clear, expressive lines like traditional Burmese newspaper cartoons
- **Watercolor wash coloring** — soft, slightly uneven, organic hand-painted feel
- **Expressive character designs** — big eyes, dramatic eyebrows, over-the-top reactions
- **Color palette**: warm beige/cream paper tones, natural skin tones, soft pastel clothing colors
- **Shading**: ink wash or loose sketch lines — no smooth digital gradients
- **Speech bubbles**: hand-drawn white ovals with thin black outlines
- **Yellow title banner** at the very top with bold black Burmese text
- **Artist signature** in bottom-right corner (small handwritten-style mark)
- **Overall feel**: like a page from a Burmese comic magazine or a newspaper editorial cartoon

---

## 2. Character Design Rules (Always Fixed)

### General Character Traits
- **Big expressive eyes** — round, wide-set, with clear pupils and dramatic eyebrows
- **Simple rounded heads** — no sharp angles, soft chin lines
- **Minimal facial details** — small dot nose, expressive mouth shapes
- **Body proportions**: slightly oversized heads, compact bodies — typical cartoon proportion
- **Skin tone**: warm light-brown / peach
- **Thanaka** — traditional yellowish-white cosmetic paste on cheeks (圆形 or leaf-shaped patterns) — a signature Burmese detail
- **Clothing**: casual, colorful patterns (longyi, plaid, floral, striped)

### Human Characters — Burmese People
- **Hair**: black, styled naturally (buns, short cuts, tied back)
- **Traditional/casual wear**: longyi (sarong-style wrap), casual shirts, sandals (rubber slippers)
- **Facial features**: warm smiles, rounded faces, highly expressive eyebrows
- **Posture**: relaxed, approachable, often sitting on low stools or floor cushions
- **Accessories**: shoulder bags (typically worn across chest), umbrellas, thanaka on cheeks

### Animal Characters — Don't Care Attitude
- **Facial expression**: deadpan, unimpressed, sleepy, or completely indifferent
- **Posture**: any random or lazy posture — sprawled out, sitting oddly, staring into space, licking a paw
- **Contrast**: the more dramatic the news, the more casually the animal reacts
- **Placement**: anywhere in the scene — foreground, background, on furniture, upside down
- **Drawing style**: simple line art, same ink outline style as human characters

---

## 3. Environment (Adapts to News Topic)

The environment changes based on the news story. Settings should feel distinctly Burmese:

- **Office / Co-working** — desks with stacked papers, ceiling fan, Myanmar calendar on wall
- **Tea shop / Cafe** — low plastic tables and stools, tea pots, small glasses, street view
- **Hospital / Clinic** — waiting bench, medicine cabinet, stethoscope, Myanmar health posters
- **Classroom / School** — wooden desks, chalkboard with Burmese writing, school uniforms
- **Living room / Home** — woven bamboo mat, low table, TV, family photos on wall
- **Street market / Shop** — canopy, hanging goods, wooden cart, customers browsing
- **Server room / Office IT** — racks, cables, fans, monitors with error screens
- **Any setting that matches the news context** — add local Burmese details

### Background Elements (Adapt Per Scene)
Include 3-5 background props relevant to the setting. Keep background simple — line art style.

---

## 4. Characters (Adapt to News Topic)

Create 2-5 characters that fit the news context:

- **Office workers** — longyi or shirt, shoulder bag, ID badge
- **Tea shop customers** — casual clothes, drinking tea, reading news
- **Doctors / Nurses / Patients** — white coat, stethoscope, patient gown
- **Students / Teachers** — white shirt + green longyi (school uniform), books
- **IT staff / Tech workers** — glasses, polo shirt, laptop bag
- **General public** — everyday Burmese clothing
- **Robot / AI character** — optional for tech news (white dome head, blue LED eyes, friendly)

At least one character should react dramatically. One animal should be completely unfazed.

## 6. Speech Bubble Rules

- **Shape**: Rounded white ovals with thin black ink stroke — slightly irregular, hand-drawn looking
- **Tail**: Points toward the speaking character's mouth
- **Text**: Bold black Burmese font, centered inside bubble
- **Placement**: Above or beside the speaker's head
- **Quantity**: 2-5 speech bubbles per scene
- **Content**: Humorous Burmese dialogue related to the news topic

---

## 7. Mood & Tone Guidelines

- **Humorous but wholesome** — funny situation, not mean-spirited
- **Light satire** on the news topic
- **At least one character reacts dramatically** — shock, laughter, facepalm
- **At least one animal is completely unbothered** — comedic contrast
- **No anger or conflict** between characters — humor comes from the situation
- **Warm, inviting atmosphere** regardless of the setting


---

## 9. What NOT To Do

- ❌ No smooth digital gradients or 3D rendering
- ❌ No photorealistic style or airbrushed shading
- ❌ No anime or manga style (big sparkly eyes, spiky hair)
- ❌ No harsh neon or saturated colors — stick to watercolor palette
- ❌ No complex architectural backgrounds — keep line art simple
- ❌ No aggressive or angry character expressions
- ❌ No heavy political caricature or mean-spirited humor
- ❌ Don't reuse the same family in every cartoon — adapt characters to the news
- ❌ Don't make animals overly expressive — they should be indifferent
